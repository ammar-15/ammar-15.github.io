# Finding the tech scene in toronto

I moved to toronto and within a few days I was already **looking for events to attend**.

One of the biggest reasons I wanted to move here was *opportunity*. not just applying for more jobs, but actually being around people **building things, starting businesses, working in tech, marketing, consulting, logistics, ecommerce, and everything in between**.

So when I saw an **AI for Ecommerce event at Stripe**, I signed up.

*Pretty solid event to attend during my first week in the city.*

## Getting into the community

One thing I noticed pretty quickly was **how different the scale is here**.

Before the event properly started, everyone introduced themselves and talked a little about what they do.

There were **founders, developers, marketers, ecommerce people, consultants, business owners, a student**, and people experimenting with AI in different parts of their work.

Some people were building their own products, some were helping other companies grow, and others were working somewhere between **tech and business**.

That part was honestly just as interesting to me as the presentations.

I've always had different interests myself. software, marketing, content creation, hospitality, and random side projects. so it was cool meeting people who also didn't necessarily fit into one specific box.

*Toronto seems to have a lot more of that.*

## Agentic commerce

A big part of the event was around **agentic commerce**.

Basically, instead of AI only helping you find something to buy, it could eventually **handle more of the process for you**.

Let's say you want a laptop.

Right now, you might ask ChatGPT for recommendations, open five tabs, compare prices, read reviews, decide on one, go to the website and buy it yourself.

With agentic commerce, you could eventually say something like:

*"Find me the best laptop under $1,500 for video editing and buy it."*

The AI could potentially find the products, compare them, choose one based on what you asked for, and handle the checkout.

*That's where things start getting interesting for ecommerce.*

## What happens to the customer journey?

One of the points that stood out to me was **how the relationship between brands and customers could change**.

Traditional ecommerce is usually pretty straightforward.

**Customer → brand**

You discover Nike, go to Nike's website, look through products and buy something.

But if AI agents become part of that process, it could look more like:

**Customer → AI agent → brand**

The AI agent could become the thing managing a lot of the shopping experience.

Which raises a pretty interesting question.

**What happens if customers stop visiting websites as much?**

Brands spend a lot of time making websites look good, improving checkout, running ads, doing SEO and trying to get people onto their site.

But if someone starts their shopping journey inside ChatGPT, Gemini or another AI tool, **that entire funnel starts changing**.

Your product still needs to be discoverable, but maybe not in the same way it is today.

## AI doesn't kill branding

At first it sounds like this could make branding less important.

*I don't think it does.*

AI can compare prices and specs, but people don't only buy things because one product has better numbers.

**People buy Nike because it's Nike.**

**People buy Apple because it's Apple.**

There's trust, reputation, marketing, community and the feeling around the product.

So brands might eventually have to think about two things:

**How do we appeal to the customer?**

And **how do we make sure AI systems can actually understand and recommend our products?**

That second part is something businesses probably haven't had to think about much before.

## Selling through AI

Stripe also talked about **building infrastructure around this**.

Because if AI agents are going to buy things for people, there's obviously a lot that needs to happen behind the scenes.

Payments, fraud protection, permissions, checkout, product information and making sure the AI is actually allowed to spend someone's money.

*You can't exactly just give an AI your credit card and say good luck.*

Stripe's approach seems to be around letting businesses publish their products across AI agents while still keeping their existing systems and infrastructure.

Which makes sense.

Brands probably aren't going to rebuild everything just because people start shopping through AI.

## AI and marketing

There was also discussion around **AI tools being used for marketing and content**.

Things like product images, video, music, avatars, localization and generating more creative variations.

This is probably the part i'm most familiar with because i've been doing more content creation myself.

AI makes it **ridiculously easy to make things now**.

But that also means everyone else can make things ridiculously easily too.

So I don't think the advantage is just being able to generate more content.

**The idea still matters.**

**The story still matters.**

**Knowing who you're talking to still matters.**

You can generate 50 ads in a day, but if none of them are interesting, *you still have 50 bad ads.*

## Meeting people

Afterwards, I got to talk to a bunch of people there.

**Founders building businesses.**

People working in consulting.

People in ecommerce.

People helping companies implement AI.

And just people working across different parts of Toronto's tech scene.

*This was probably my favourite part.*

When you move somewhere new, you don't really know what the scene looks like yet.

You can see companies on LinkedIn and apply for jobs online, but **talking to people gives you a much better idea of what people are actually working on**.

Someone might be running an ecommerce business.

Someone else is consulting for companies.

Another person is building a startup on the side.

Another works a normal job but is experimenting with AI tools for their company.

*There's a lot happening.*

## Why i moved

This is basically **one of the reasons I moved to Toronto**.

I can code from anywhere.

I can apply for jobs from anywhere.

I can make content from anywhere.

But it's harder to recreate being able to **find an event, take a train downtown and suddenly be in a room full of people working on completely different things**.

I've only been here for a few days and i'm still figuring out the city.

*But this was a pretty good start.*

Hopefully there are **a lot more events like this coming up**.
